Privacy Policy — HAM Signal

Effective Date: March 25, 2026  ·  Last Updated: October 9, 2026

In short: HAM Signal has no accounts, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking, and there is no HAM Signal server. Your settings and logbook stay on your devices and in your own iCloud. The app contacts public amateur-radio and space-weather services directly. A few of those requests include your callsign or approximate location, and each one is listed below.

Introduction

This policy explains what the HAM Signal app for iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch (including its widgets) stores, what it sends over the internet and to whom, and how you can control it. "We" means the developer of HAM Signal.

Contact: stephenlthorn@gmail.com

1. What We Collect

We collect nothing. HAM Signal does not send any information to us. It contains no analytics, crash-reporting, advertising or tracking software. It does not use your data to track you across other companies' apps or websites, and we do not sell or share your data.

2. What Stays on Your Device

  • Settings and profile: your callsign, license class, home location (QTH) and grid square, alert rules, saved repeaters and preferences.
  • Logbook: the contacts (QSOs) you log, stored on your device and synced to your own iCloud Drive (see section 4).
  • Cached data: the latest solar, propagation, satellite and spot data, kept so the app, widgets and Apple Watch can work offline.
  • Notifications: alerts for space weather, band openings, DX spots and skeds are created by the app on your device. No notification server is involved.
  • Calculations: propagation estimates, greyline, satellite pass predictions and Maidenhead grid conversions are all computed on your device.

The widgets and the Apple Watch app get their data from the iPhone app through Apple's on-device app group and Watch connectivity, and from the public data sources listed in section 3.

3. Location

Location access is optional and only used while the app is open ("When In Use"). If you allow it, your location is used to centre the maps, work out your grid square, and estimate local propagation, greyline and satellite passes. These calculations happen on your device. You can enter your location by hand instead, and you can turn location access off at any time in iOS Settings › Privacy & Security › Location Services.

Only an approximate location ever leaves your device, in two cases: APRS sends your location rounded to about 10 km to the APRS-IS network to find nearby stations, and Band Activity sends your 4-character grid square (an area roughly 110 × 160 km) to PSK Reporter. Both are explained in section 4.

4. Services the App Contacts

HAM Signal connects directly to the public services below. We don't run any of them. Each one may record ordinary connection information such as your IP address under its own policy. Unless stated otherwise, connections use HTTPS.

Space weather and satellite data (no personal data sent)

  • NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center (services.swpc.noaa.gov): solar flux, sunspot regions, K and A indices, X-ray and proton flux, forecasts and solar imagery.
  • CelesTrak (celestrak.org): orbital data for amateur-radio satellites.

APRS (APRS-IS network)

  • Sent: when you open the APRS tab, the app connects to the volunteer-run APRS-IS network (rotate.aprs.net, port 14580) and asks for stations near your location, rounded to about 10 km. By default it connects anonymously and in receive-only mode, as N0CALL, so your callsign is not sent. Your callsign and a matching APRS-IS passcode are sent only if you turn on "Log in as [your callsign]" in the APRS tab, which first shows exactly what will be sent. The app never transmits a position beacon.
  • Earlier versions: HAM Signal 3.5 and earlier always logged in with the callsign you entered and a matching passcode, and sent your location rounded to about 100 metres.
  • Note: APRS-IS connections are plain text and not encrypted. APRS is a public amateur-radio network.

DX cluster

  • Sent: when you use the DX Cluster tab, the app logs in to a public DX cluster node (dxc.nc7j.com, dxc.ve7cc.net or w3lpl.net) with your callsign, because cluster nodes require one.
  • Note: DX cluster connections are plain-text telnet and are not encrypted.

PSK Reporter ("Heard" and Band Activity)

  • Heard: your callsign is sent to retrieve.pskreporter.info to find which stations have reported hearing you. Your location is not sent.
  • Band Activity: your 4-character grid square (for example FN31, an area roughly 110 × 160 km) is sent, to find DX stations being heard near you. Your callsign and exact location are not sent. HAM Signal 3.5 and earlier sent nothing about you here and requested a worldwide feed instead.

Callsign lookups

  • callook.info (FCC license data): the callsign you look up, such as your own when checking your license class, or a contact's when logging a QSO.
  • HamDB (api.hamdb.org): the callsigns of APRS stations you look up or add to your list.

Parks on the Air (POTA) and Summits on the Air (SOTA)

  • Park and summit lookups: the park or summit reference you enter, sent to api.pota.app or api2.sota.org.uk.
  • Self-spots: only when you post a spot. The app sends your callsign, the park reference, frequency, mode and your comment to POTA (api.pota.app). POTA spots are public and anyone can see them.

Apple

  • iCloud Drive: your logbook is stored in HAM Signal's folder in your own iCloud Drive, so it syncs between your devices. We cannot access it. To stop the sync, turn off iCloud Drive for HAM Signal in iOS Settings › [your name] › iCloud.
  • In-app purchases: HAM Signal Pro is purchased through Apple's App Store. Apple handles payment. The app only receives confirmation of your purchase and never sees your payment details.
  • See Apple's Privacy Policy.

Links you open

Some screens link to websites such as QRZ.com or RepeaterBook. These open in your browser only when you tap them, and that website's policy then applies.

5. Your Choices and Rights

  • Location: allow, deny or revoke at any time in iOS Settings.
  • Notifications: turn them off in the app's alert settings or in iOS Settings › Notifications.
  • Network features: APRS, DX cluster, Heard, lookups and POTA spotting only connect when you use them. If you don't open those features, nothing involving your callsign or location is sent.
  • Delete your data: Settings › Clear Cached Solar Data removes cached data. Deleting the app removes everything stored on the device. To delete the synced logbook, remove HAM Signal's data in iOS Settings › [your name] › iCloud › Manage Account Storage.

We hold no personal data about you, so there is nothing for us to access, correct or delete. Data posted to public networks (for example, a POTA spot) is controlled by those networks. If you live in the EEA, UK or California and have questions about your rights, contact stephenlthorn@gmail.com.

6. Children's Privacy

HAM Signal is not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect any data from children.

7. Changes to This Policy

If what the app sends changes, we will update this page and the "Last Updated" date above.

8. Space Weather Disclaimer

HAM Signal shows space-weather data from NOAA and other public sources for informational purposes only. We do not guarantee its accuracy, completeness or timeliness, and conditions can change quickly. Do not rely solely on this app for decisions that could affect the safety of life or property. HAM Signal is not affiliated with or endorsed by NOAA, NASA, ARRL, POTA, SOTA, PSK Reporter or any APRS-IS or DX cluster operator.
